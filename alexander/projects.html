TRACES 🔗

Interactive installation · 2026

Concept

Contamination is not an event. It is a process, and TRACES gives it a room. Visitors aim weak coloured lasers at a 3D-printed alpine terrain, and wherever a beam lands, water of that colour enters the world. It runs downhill, takes on the colours it meets on the way, and settles into a projected sea at the foot of the installation. The sea keeps everything as sediment. Time is compressed, so nothing is ever quite gone: an input thins to a line, then lies under newer layers, still legible. It plays like a game. The sea keeps score.

Form

The terrain is the route a persistent chemical takes. What enters at the summit goes down, into valleys, into rivers, into the sea. Water is the medium because water is how PFAS travel, and because a local emission needs a route in order to become a global one. The laser is the gesture: we aim from a safe distance and the damage collects at our feet. Permanence becomes stratigraphy. Real PFAS settle into sediment cores that scientists read as an archive of what was released, and the simulated sea is read the same way.

Result

By the end of the exhibition the sea holds hundreds of decisions. None of them is attributable, all of them are there. An archive of distributed responsibility.

Chilled Coffee Cat 🔗

Pinsel und Pixel · 2026 · with Sandra Schindler

Concept

A cat, painted twice. First digitally, in layers. Then the image is thrown onto canvas and followed in acrylic, brushstroke for brushstroke. What is left is a hand-painted cat that still knows, to within a millimetre, which layer each of its marks came from.

Form

That correspondence turns the canvas back into a screen. The digital layers drive animation across the painted surface: light, shadow, movement, all of it returned to the brushstrokes it came from.

Result

The first work in the series Pinsel und Pixel. Acrylic on canvas, and the canvas does not know it is finished.

Shrocket 🔗

DIY electronics · 2026 · with Clemens Grünewald

Concept

A soldering kit is a bag of parts, a board and a set of instructions. You work through the steps, learn something with your hands, and end up with an object for your desk. The usual ones are animals or platonic solids. This one is a mushroom rocket.

Form

One board, cut to the silhouette of a mushroom rocket. The spots on the cap are bare substrate: no mask, no copper, just the material itself, left thin enough for the light of an LED to pass through. Under the cap the traces fan outward and read as gills. The exhaust flame at the base is lit the same way. Nothing here is decoration that is not also circuit.

Result

A soldering kit for people who want something on their desk that has to be explained.

Can be found here: Binary Kitchen/Soldering Tutorial. A ready-made kit is available at blinkyparts.

Fairydust 🔗

Installation · 2023

Concept

A wooden rocket, lit by a projector instead of by LEDs. Fairydust is a relief on a wall and holds no colour of its own. Everything it shows is thrown at it. A gradient walks through its layers, one hue per layer at a time. Beside it, an exhaust plume hits the wall and stays there.

Form

The body is stacked wooden layers, each cut from the same CAD model the projection is mapped to, so the object and its image are one geometry. TouchDesigner drives the light live: one hue per layer, a gradient across them, a slow drift over time, all of it out of low-frequency three-dimensional perlin noise. The plume is a fluid simulation, aimed at the adjacent wall.

Result

A quiet piece. People stop in front of it without deciding to.

Festival Totem 🔗

Interactive object · 2022 · with Thomas Schmid

Concept

A beacon for places where phones give up. An egg-shaped lamp on a pole, a metre above the crowd. It listens to whatever is playing and answers in light: every second bar a wave runs down the shell, and on a drop a torch flares inside, the bass feeding the fire. To change mode the carrier knocks the pole twice on the ground. There are no other controls. You find the egg in order to find the people.

Form

A lamp on a pole: an old idea, built cleanly. Height beats line of sight, and brightness multiplies. The object listens to the crowd, not to whoever is carrying it. The egg is its own lampshade, printed in vase mode from translucent PLA.

Result

A festival fills with totems, one to a crew. Together they draw a map of where everybody is, in light, for as long as the music holds.
